01

Your profit is a lie (until you subtract fees)

You buy a hoodie for $168, sell it for $240, and tell yourself you made $72. You didn't. After the platform's cut, payment processing, and the label you paid for, your real take-home is closer to $45 — and if you listed it on the wrong platform, under $10.

The sellers who last aren't the ones with the best eye. They're the ones who know their real number before they buy.

Fees moved a lot in the last two years — Mercari shifted its cut back to sellers, Depop and Vinted dropped seller fees, Whatnot layered commission on top of processing. Most "fee guides" online still quote 2022 numbers. Here's where it actually stands, and how to price around it.

07

The hidden costs that quietly eat you

Fees are the obvious cut. These are the ones that don't show up in the headline rate but show up in your bank account.

  • Promoted / boosted listings. eBay Promoted, Depop Boost, Poshmark & Mercari promote — all take an extra cut on top of fees, often 2–12%+. Turn them on deliberately, not by default.
  • Shipping you eat. A $9 label on a $35 item is 26% gone before any fee. Know for each platform whether you or the buyer pays, and price accordingly.
  • Returns & refunds. Some platforms refund your fees on a return, some don't. You may also eat return shipping and get an item back in worse condition. Build a small return rate into your pricing.
  • Offers & coupons. "Accept offer," "offer to likers," auto-coupons — every one is a discount off your margin, not the platform's.
  • Crosslisting tools & supplies. Apps like Vendoo or List Perfectly run $10–40/mo. Poly mailers, boxes, tape, thermal labels, ink — all real costs. All deductible (see Guide 02).

Every one of these is a line you should be logging in the tracker. Untracked costs don't just hurt your margin — they become income you pay tax on.

09

When your fees drop with volume

The headline rates aren't fixed forever. As you scale, some platforms quietly get cheaper — and knowing the break points keeps you from overpaying.

  • eBay Store subscription. Once you're listing steadily (roughly 50+ items/month), a Store plan ($4.99–$59.95/mo) cuts your final value fee and hands you hundreds of free listings. Past a certain volume it pays for itself several times over.
  • StockX & GOAT seller levels. Both lower your transaction % as your lifetime sales climb. A brand-new StockX seller pays the top rate; a high-volume one pays noticeably less on every sale.
  • Flat 10–20% platforms (Mercari, Poshmark). No real volume discount — the rate is the rate. Scale here comes from speed and quantity, not cheaper fees.

The takeaway: if one platform is becoming your main channel, check whether a subscription or seller tier would lower your rate before you do another month at full price.

10

The rule that stops you selling at a loss

Before you buy anything to flip, run this. It gives you the lowest price you can accept and still hit your target profit — so you never get talked down into a bad deal.

Minimum list price
( your cost + shipping + target profit ) ÷ ( 1 − fee% )

Example: a sneaker cost you $65, $8 to ship, you want at least $25 profit, selling on eBay (≈14% all-in). Minimum = ($65 + $8 + $25) ÷ 0.86 = $114. List below that and you're working for free.

Faster rule of thumb: on a 10–14% fee platform you keep roughly 85¢ of every dollar, so to cover cost you need to sell for at least cost ÷ 0.85 — and that's just break-even, before a cent of profit.

14

Sales tax: what you actually pay

Good news first: in almost every US state, the marketplace collects and remits sales tax for you (it's called marketplace facilitator law). The buyer pays it at checkout, the platform sends it to the state. It is not a cost that comes out of your pocket, and it's not your profit.

What you do owe is income tax on your profit — sale price minus what you paid minus your fees and expenses. That's a completely different thing, and it's where most resellers overpay by not tracking their costs. Platforms may send you a 1099-K; as of 2025–26 the federal reporting threshold is back up to $20,000 and 200+ transactions, though some states are lower — and the threshold only affects reporting, not whether you owe.

This is the one that costs people the most, so it gets its own guide → Guide 02: Reseller Tax & Deductions.

15

Margin rules worth tattooing on your arm

  • Aim for 50%+ ROI, not a fixed dollar amount. Flipping $65 into $100 beats flipping $600 into $650 — same $35, far less cash at risk and faster to repeat.
  • Cheap items hide fees. A $12 sale can lose 20%+ to flat fees alone. Bundle small items, or sell them where flat fees are low.
  • Shipping is a cost, not an afterthought. A $9 label turns a "profit" into a loss on anything under ~$40. Price it in or make the buyer pay.
  • Tied-up cash is real. Three items unsold for 60 days is money you can't reinvest. Track days-to-sell, not just profit.
  • Track every fee the day it happens. At tax time, every fee you logged is a deduction. Every one you forgot is profit you'll pay tax on.
  • Speed beats a few extra dollars. A fast, certain sale at 40% ROI usually beats holding for a "maybe" 60%. Cash that moves compounds.

That fifth rule is why this kit comes with a tracker — fees you don't record become taxable income you didn't actually keep.
