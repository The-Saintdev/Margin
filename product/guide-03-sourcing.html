01

Your profit is set at the buy

New resellers obsess over selling — better photos, better listings, better prices. That stuff matters at the edges. But the truth the pros know is simpler: the margin is locked in the moment you buy. You cannot sell your way out of overpaying.

Discipline at the buy beats hustle at the sale. A great sourcer with mediocre listings still wins. A great seller who overpays loses.

So this guide is mostly about one skill: knowing — before money leaves your hand — whether an item will actually make you money. Everything else is logistics.

02

The 30-second sourcing check

Run this on your phone, standing in the aisle, before every buy. If you can't answer all three, put it down.

  1. What did it actually SELL for? Not the asking price — the sold price. On eBay, filter to "Sold items." On sneakers, check StockX/GOAT last sales. Asking prices are fantasy; sold prices are facts.
  2. How often does it sell? Look at how many sold recently vs how many are listed. Ten sold this month and five listed = liquid. Two sold this year and 90 listed = you'll be holding it forever.
  3. After fees and my cost, do I keep enough? Run the resale price through the break-even multiplier from Guide 01. If it doesn't clear your target profit, it's not a deal — no matter how "cheap" it feels.

The discipline: "it's only $5" is not a reason to buy. "It sells for $40, moves weekly, and I keep $25 after fees" is. Feelings lose money; comps make it.

03

Sell-through beats "it's worth a lot"

Sell-through rate = how many of a thing sold versus how many are listed, over a period. It's the single most underrated number in reselling, because it tells you whether your money comes back.

A quick way to eyeball it on eBay: compare the count of sold listings to active listings for the same item. Lots sold, few listed → high sell-through → cash moves fast. Few sold, tons listed → you're item #91 in line.

Why it matters more than price
$30 profit every week ≫ $200 profit once a year

Cash that moves compounds. A fast-selling $30 flip you can repeat 50 times a year makes far more than a prestigious $200 item gathering dust. Chase velocity, not just margin.

05

What holds value — and what doesn't

Some things reliably resell; some are traps that feel like finds. General patterns — markets shift, so always comp.

✓ Tends to flip well

  • Sneakers — Jordan, Nike SB, limited Dunks, New Balance
  • Durable brands — Carhartt, Patagonia, The North Face, vintage denim
  • Lego — retired sets climb hard
  • Trading cards & sealed games — Pokémon, retro consoles
  • Power tools — DeWalt, Milwaukee, Snap-on
  • Small appliances — Dyson, KitchenAid, espresso machines
  • Vintage tees & band merch, designer archive, hype drops

✕ Usually a trap

  • Fast fashion — Shein, H&M, Forever 21: near-zero resale
  • No-name / unbranded anything you can't comp
  • Heavily faked items you can't authenticate
  • Bulky, low-value goods — shipping eats you alive
  • Fast-depreciating tech — last year's cheap phones/tablets
  • Saturated items — 200 already listed, few selling
  • Damaged goods you "can fix later" (you won't)

The through-line: brands with demand and scarcity hold value; generic, abundant, or fragile things don't. When in doubt, the sold-listings count tells you the truth.

06

The spot-math buy rule

You don't need a calculator in the aisle — you need a ceiling. Work backward from the realistic resale price (from your comps), then cap what you'll pay.

Most you should pay
realistic resale × ( 1 − fee% ) − shipping − your target profit

Fast rule of thumb for a healthy flip: pay no more than a third to a half of what it realistically resells for. A jacket that sells for $60 on a ~13% platform, $9 to ship, $25 target profit → pay at most about $18. Over that, walk away.

This is the same break-even math as Guide 01, just run in reverse — resale first, costs out, and whatever's left is your hard ceiling. Write your ceiling before you negotiate, and hold it.

07

Authentication & avoiding fakes

Sneakers, designer, trading cards, and electronics are flooded with convincing fakes. Selling one — even unknowingly — gets you banned and refunded. Protect yourself:

  • Learn one category deeply before you spend real money in it. Know the tells: stitching, tags, box labels, weight, fonts.
  • Use authenticated platforms (StockX, GOAT) for hype items — they verify before the buyer gets it. You pay more in fees for that protection; often worth it.
  • If you're not sure, it's a no. A passed-up real item costs nothing. A fake costs your money, your account, and your rep.
  • Keep proof of purchase for anything high-value — receipts settle disputes.
08

Timing & seasonality

The same item is worth different amounts in different months. Buy when nobody wants it, sell when everybody does.

  • Buy off-season, sell in-season. Coats and boots: buy in spring, sell in fall. Grills and patio: buy in fall, sell in spring.
  • Holidays spike demand. Toys, collectibles, and gifts peak Oct–Dec. Costumes spike before Halloween.
  • Hype drops have a clock. Sneaker and streetwear resale is hottest right after a sold-out drop, then cools. Move fast.
  • Tax-refund season (Feb–Apr) puts cash in buyers' pockets — big-ticket items move better.
09

Rookie mistakes that cost money

  • Buying off the asking price, not the sold price. The #1 beginner mistake.
  • Emotional buys — "this is so cool" is not "this will sell."
  • Ignoring fees and shipping until the sale, then realizing you lost money.
  • Hoarding slow sellers — cash tied up in dead inventory can't buy winners.
  • Going high-ticket with no cushion — one $600 flip that won't sell can freeze your whole operation.
  • Not listing fast. An item in a pile earns nothing. Source it, list it within 48 hours, log it.
10

A repeatable weekly routine

Winning resellers aren't lucky — they run a loop. Here's a simple one:

  1. Set a sourcing budget you can afford to tie up — and stick to it.
  2. Source on a schedule — pick your sources and go weekly, not randomly.
  3. Run the 30-second check on every item. Comp, sell-through, margin. No comp, no buy.
  4. List within 48 hours — and log every buy in the tracker so you always know your real numbers.
  5. Reinvest profit, not rent. Roll winnings back into inventory; take out only what's yours to keep.
  6. Niche down to what you know best — depth in one category beats dabbling in ten.

Every buy you log in Margin sharpens the next one: you'll see which categories, sources, and price points actually profit for you — and double down on them.

A note on expectations. Resale markets shift constantly — what flips well this year may cool next year, and prices vary by region and season. Everything here is general guidance to build judgment, not a guarantee of profit. Always verify current sold prices before you buy, and start small while you learn a category.
